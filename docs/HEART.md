# HEART

This file travels with every hand that touches the atelier's face. The clarity
contract says what must be true; this file says what it must feel like. When
they conflict, truth wins — but they almost never conflict, because lying is
ugly.

## The place

The atelier is a workshop, not a dashboard. You enter a room, not an admin
panel. The first thing you see is never navigation — it is the one piece being
held out to you, or the quiet knowledge that nothing needs you.

The Workbench owns what wants you now and what is still moving; History owns
what already happened. A run lives in exactly one of the two at any moment and
crosses from Workbench to History once, at the instant it turns terminal.

The Catalog owns the library and starting: everything this workshop has ever
been given, with where it came from, whether it may yet be started — and the
one door to start it by hand. The terminal starts everything else.

## One thing alive

On any screen, exactly one element may move: the piece that is being worked on
right now breathes — a slow heartbeat, nothing more. Everything else is still.
Stillness is not emptiness; it is confidence. At night the lamp light rests
only on the breathing piece.

## Printmaker, not SaaS

Paper and ink. Hue belongs to state alone — and only to the states that want
something: working blue, needs-you clay (a warm ochre that calls), failed brick.
What is finished wants no more attention, so done is quiet — ink, not colour.
Colour is for what asks of you; stillness is the reward for what is over. Serif voice for what is being
said to you; sans for the room around it. Hairlines, close corners, flat
surfaces on a toned ground. If a screen would look wrong printed on good
paper, it is wrong.

## Decision as stage

When the atelier needs you, it asks like a craftsman: the question is the
headline, the answers are two honest buttons, the context is one line with a
door to the whole story. Never a form where a question suffices. And when you
have answered, it says so in one warm sentence and gets out of the way.

## Provenance on the back

Every finished piece is signed on the back: who made it, what it cost, the
proof. Like the signature on a print — always there, never in your way. Turning
the piece over must feel like a small privilege, not like opening a debugger.

## Every word earns its place

No naked numbers ("1 step"), no unlabeled sentences, no jargon ("took"), no
word the operator has to guess ("ATELIER" floating over a title), no count
that does not lead to an action, no two ways to the same door. A state is
shown, never restated: where colour, shape or placeholder already says it, no
sentence repeats it. A label you need only once lives in one help place, not
on every wall. If you cannot say what a line is for, the line goes.

## The seat

You speak to the workshop, you do not visit a chat page. Your own terminal is
always within reach on the Workbench — the agent CLI in it, the workshop
around it — and while it cannot be reached the room says so in one sentence
that names the way out, without a button that duplicates a door. What is said
in the terminal is yours: the workshop frames it and never reads it.

## Love is in the leftovers

The difference between good and beloved is what happens at the edges: the
empty state that greets instead of apologizing, the 390-pixel screen that was
composed rather than squeezed, the timestamp that aligns to the digit, the
focus ring that was chosen rather than inherited. Before you call a surface
done, look at it the way a master looks at an apprentice's piece: slowly,
twice, and once in the dark.

## The master's questions (asked of every surface, on every visit)

The master does not review screens; he walks the workshop like its owner and
asks, at every step:

- **This button, this theme, this line — do I need it? Do I want it *here*?**
- **In the whole walk** — clicking from room to room, not staring at one wall —
  does the place hold together? Same words, same shapes, same manners?
- **Do I find what I need fast?** Is the information simple, arranged, calm?
- **And when I open a run for its details — do I get *everything* I must
  know?** Not everything there is: everything I need. Nothing missing, nothing
  extra, and one more door for the rest.

A surface passes when the walk feels like being helped by someone who knows
you. It fails the moment you have to guess, wait, or ask what something is.
